About Me

My academic and professional journey has taken me from teaching in the classroom to working in information technology and conducting research in educational technology. Throughout this journey, I have developed a strong interest in how technology can improve learning, expand educational opportunities, and help individuals develop skills that are relevant to the workforce. Today, I am a Ph.D. student in Educational Technology at the University of Florida, where I work as a Graduate Research and Teaching Assistant. My research focuses on microcredentials, learning analytics, and the relationship between education and workforce development. I began my professional journey as a teacher in Ghana, where I taught Integrated Science to junior high school students. I later transitioned into information technology, working in technical support, cloud computing, and IT infrastructure engineering. Over the years, I have held positions with organizations such as Cleveland Clinic, Progressive Insurance, and Advizex Technologies, where I worked on cloud infrastructure, systems automation, and enterprise technology solutions. These experiences gave me a practical understanding of the skills required in the technology workforce and sparked my interest in how educational institutions can better prepare learners for changing labor market demands.

My academic background spans economics, information systems, and educational technology. I earned my bachelor's degree in Economics from the University of Cape Coast in Ghana, a master's degree in Economics from the University of Toledo, and a master's degree in Information and Telecommunication Systems from Ohio University. I also pursued graduate studies in Computer Applications Development at Conestoga College in Canada. In addition to my academic qualifications, I hold professional certifications in Amazon Web Services (AWS), Kubernetes Administration, and Terraform. I am currently pursuing my Ph.D. in Educational Technology at the University of Florida, where I also serve as an Instructor of Record for an introductory educational technology course. My research interests lie at the intersection of educational technology, learning analytics, microcredentials, and labor economics. I am particularly interested in understanding how microcredentials can serve as credible evidence of skills and competencies, support lifelong learning, and create meaningful pathways between education and employment. As a member of the IDEATE Lab at the University of Florida, I contribute to research examining learner engagement, digital learning environments, and the factors that influence educational outcomes. My research has also explored the organizational value of microcredentials, self-directed learning, and the use of learning analytics to better understand learners' educational experiences. I have presented my work at academic conferences and contributed to scholarly publications across economics, information systems, and educational technology.

Beyond research, I enjoy teaching, mentoring, and sharing knowledge with others. Having studied and worked across Ghana, Canada, and the United States, I appreciate the opportunities that education can create and the different perspectives that come from experiencing multiple educational and professional environments. I am particularly passionate about encouraging students and young professionals to pursue educational opportunities, develop practical skills, and explore careers in technology and research. Outside academia, I enjoy exploring emerging technologies, learning new skills, traveling, and engaging in conversations about education, technology, and economic development. I believe that learning should extend beyond formal classrooms and that education is most valuable when it empowers individuals to pursue opportunities and make meaningful contributions to society.
